## 1. How a transformer turns text into a vector

1. The text is split into word pieces (tokens), at most 256 per text.
2. Each token starts as a learned **word embedding**: a vector that places words with similar use
   close together.
3. Six **self-attention** layers let every token look at every other token and mix in what is
   relevant. "Silk" next to "art" ends up different from "silk" next to "pure".
4. The token vectors are averaged (mean pooling) and scaled to length 1. Cosine similarity between
   two texts is then a single dot product.

TF-IDF only counts words. Two texts with no word in common score 0, whatever they mean.

## 2. Same meaning, different words

| Pair | MiniLM cosine | TF-IDF cosine |
|---|---|---|
| saree – sari | **0.733** | 0.000 |
| handwoven – handloom | **0.633** | 0.000 |
| keychain – key ring | **0.622** | 0.000 |
| silk saree – cotton saree | 0.754 | 0.334 |
| dupatta – stole | 0.230 | 0.000 |
| earrings – jhumka | 0.157 | 0.000 |
| saree – keychain (unrelated) | 0.115 | 0.000 |
| necklace – cushion cover (unrelated) | 0.175 | 0.000 |

The embedding knows English synonyms and spelling variants (sari / saree, key ring / keychain).
It knows **Indian craft terms poorly**: a jhumka *is* a kind of earring, but it scores near an
unrelated pair. MiniLM was trained mostly on general English web text.

## 3. Similar-items search: LSA vs transformer

Same FAISS exact search as part 11, 10 nearest training listings, median price as the estimate:

| Representation | Test R² | MAE ₹ | Mean top-10 similarity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LSA: TF-IDF + SVD, fitted on this catalogue (part 11)** | **0.613** | **825** | 0.870 |
| MiniLM transformer, general English | 0.545 | 903 | 0.854 |

**The simpler, in-domain representation wins.** LSA learned this catalogue's own vocabulary
(ajrakh, bagru, zari, 3pc). MiniLM brings general knowledge but blurs the craft distinctions that
drive price. Part 18 (RAG) therefore keeps the LSA retriever.

## 4. Do the embeddings add to the best model?

PCA fitted on training rows, then added to the full S12-img feature set. Grouped 5-fold CV:

| Components added | 0 (S12-img) | 32 | **64** | 128 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CV R² | 0.7795 | 0.7787 | **0.7832** | 0.7826 |
| CV MAE ₹ | **622.5** | 627.5 | 628.2 | 626.4 |

**Decision, made on CV before scoring the test set: not adopted.** The best R² gain is +0.004,
inside the ±0.01 band that the ablation (part 8b) showed to be noise, and MAE gets slightly
worse. With TF-IDF, LLM attributes and photos already present, the sentence vector repeats what
the model knows.

**Stage S12-img-st, scored once for the record** (64 components):

| Stage | Ridge R² | MAE ₹ | MAPE % | Band F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S12-img (final)** | **0.806** | **612** | **34.2** | 0.821 |
| S12-img-st | 0.804 | 619 | 34.7 | 0.824 |

The test agrees with the CV decision: no real gain. S12-img remains the final model.

## Notes

- **Crash and fix:** the first run crashed (exit 139, a segmentation fault) as soon as PyTorch and
  FAISS were both loaded. Each ships its own OpenMP threading library, and on macOS the two clash.
  Setting thread variables didn't help, so the script now runs as two processes: `encode`
  (PyTorch only) and `evaluate` (FAISS + scikit-learn only).
- **Why still worth having:** this part shows the transformer and word-embedding ideas working
  (section 2), and gives a measured answer to "would a modern embedding help here?": not on this
  catalogue, and the report explains why.
